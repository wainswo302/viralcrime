# Editorial, Disclosure &amp; Corrections Policy

*This page is public-facing. It is both a trust signal (Google E-E-A-T for crime/safety content) and a working part of the system — the naming gate and de-indexing pipeline point here.*

---

## Who we are and what we do

We track crime-related incidents that appear in official public records and that circulate on social media, from the moment they surface through to their verified resolution. Our goal is the part of the story the internet usually skips: what actually happened — the charge, the court disposition, and whether the video everyone shared was even real.

We are an editorial publication built on primary sources. We are **not** a tip line, a crowd-investigation forum, or a place to identify people the authorities have not.

## How we source

Every factual claim on an incident or case page links to a primary source — an official police or court record, or established mainstream coverage. If we cannot source a claim to a primary record, we do not publish it. Where a claim is contested or unverified, we label it as unverified rather than asserting it.

## How we handle names

This is the core of our policy, and it is enforced in our publishing system, not left to individual judgment.

We **do not name private individuals** in connection with an alleged crime. Incident pages describe the *event* — what happened, where, and the official status — without identifying private people.

A person is named **only** when all of the following are true:

1. They are a defendant of record in an adjudicated or formally charged matter — never an internet-speculated "suspect."
2. The matter is already documented in official records **and** covered by established news outlets.
3. We hold at least two independent primary sources.

If those conditions are not met, the name does not appear — not in the headline, the URL, the body, or the page's metadata. We would rather publish a less complete page than a wrong one.

## Presumption of innocence

A charge is not a conviction. Every page involving a person facing charges states the current legal status plainly and carries the presumption of innocence. We actively monitor cases for updates and revise pages as matters resolve.

## Video verification

For viral footage, we publish a verification status: authentic, miscaptioned, recirculated (real but old), staged, AI-generated, or unverified. We establish this through human-verifiable methods — source contact, geolocation, metadata, and cross-referencing. Automated AI-detection tools, where used, are one input that triggers human review; they are never the sole basis for a published verdict, because their reliability is limited and degrades quickly.

We embed original social media posts using each platform's native embed tools. We do not download, rehost, or re-upload anyone's video or images. If an original poster removes a post, it disappears from our pages too.

## Corrections

We correct errors promptly and visibly. Corrected pages carry a dated note describing what changed and why. To request a correction, email **corrections@[site]**.

## Removal, updates, and expungement

Legal outcomes change. Convictions are overturned; charges are dropped; records are sealed or expunged. When a matter is resolved in a person's favor, or a record is expunged, we update or remove the relevant page and remove it from search indexing. To request a review on these grounds, email **corrections@[site]** with a reference to the record. We take these requests seriously and act on verified ones.

## What we will not do

We will not host crowd-sourced identification of suspects, publish unverified accusations against private individuals, accept anonymous "tips" naming people, or build timelines profiling private persons. These practices cause real harm and have no place here.
